Raven Cliff Falls
5 mi out and backModerate~2.5–3 h15 min from Helen
Follows Dodd Creek past several cascades to a waterfall that pours through a split in a cliff. Start by about 1:15 to finish well before dark.

Too tired from the red-eye? Duke Creek Falls is on the same road: 2 miles round trip to a viewing platform over a tall double waterfall.

Panther Creek Falls
~7 mi out and back~650 ft gain~4 h
The long waterfall hike of the trip. The trail runs along the creek through a gorge, with smaller cascades, to a wide waterfall with a big pool at the bottom. Some narrow, rocky sections with cables.
Tallulah Gorge
2–3 mi of rim trails~1,000 stairs to the suspension bridgeState park parking fee
A 1,000-ft-deep gorge with waterfalls and overlooks along both rims. Hiking the gorge floor needs a free permit (limited each day, first come first served).
Georgia Power has historically scheduled dam water releases on November weekends. On release days the park doesn't issue gorge-floor permits, though the falls are bigger from the rim. Check the schedule, and if Sunday is a release day, swap Tallulah to Monday.

Blood Mountain loop (Appalachian Trail + Freeman Trail)
~6 mi loop~1,500 ft gain~4 hStrenuous
The highest point on Georgia's Appalachian Trail, with wide views from the summit rocks and a stone shelter on top. Going up the AT and coming back on the Freeman Trail makes a loop around the mountain. Add an out-and-back on the AT to make the day longer.
The trailhead lot holds only about 38 cars and fills on weekends, which is why this hike is on Monday. Don't park outside the marked spaces; cars there get ticketed.

Where to stay

Three nights, check in Sat Nov 21 and check out Tue Nov 24. Staying in Helen itself means dinners and takeout are walkable, nobody drives mountain roads in the dark, and every trailhead is 15–45 minutes away. Ask for 2 queen beds.
