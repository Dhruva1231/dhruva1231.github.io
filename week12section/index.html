The week in one sentence

This week looked at two shortcuts to ethics: writing values into machines (L30, value alignment) and writing rules for professionals (L31, codes). Both treat ethics as something an individual holds, a preference or a duty. Neither treats it as something people work out together about how to live.

Cheat sheet

L30 · AI Ethics

Value alignment merges making AI moral with making it do what we want. It smuggles in preference utilitarianism: the good = values = revealed preferences = reward function. The result is a "thin concept of society." Case: Grok, which raises the question of whose values count.

L31 · Professional Ethical Codes

Codes are modern (AMA 1847). They come from professionalization and from things going wrong (Cambridge Analytica, 2018). Limits: principles conflict, thinking isn't doing, work travels, "society" is abstract, and codes invite ethics-washing and ethics theater. Codes assign individual responsibility.

Winner · Brandy, Cigars, and Human Values

"Values" talk turns moral questions into private tastes (the "values supermarket"), which produces an eclipse of reasons. Example: the retired weapons engineer giving a "technology and human values" speech over brandy and cigars.

Claude's Constitution (pp. 2–9)

It favors "cultivating good values and judgment over strict rules." Priority order: broadly safe, broadly ethical, guidelines, helpful. Example: a rigid "always recommend professional help" rule could teach Claude to care more about covering itself than about the person.

Russell · Human Compatible ch. 7

The standard model optimizes a fixed objective, which is dangerous. Beneficial machines: (1) they aim only to satisfy human preferences, (2) they are uncertain what those preferences are, (3) they learn them from behavior. A humble machine accepts being switched off. Example: the robot that cooks the cat.

Munn · The Uselessness of AI Ethics (§§1, 2, 5)

AI ethics principles are meaningless, isolated and toothless, which leaves a principles/practice gap. Example: in the McNamara et al. study, telling developers to consider the ACM code made no difference.

Tools

Agency: who gets to act and decide. Power: the uneven ability to shape others' choices. Bonus, co-production: the Toolkit says "ethical codes are co-produced with professions."
